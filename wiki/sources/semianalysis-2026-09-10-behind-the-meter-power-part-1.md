Part 1 of a deep dive on **behind-the-meter (BTM) power** — on-site generation that bypasses the public grid — now mainstream for every AI lab and hyperscaler. SemiAnalysis's Energy Model tracks **75 GW of firm, binding BTM orders** (~20 GW in Q2 2026 alone; ~3 GW operational US IT capacity by year-end, then multiple years of triple-digit growth). The economics: a ~1 GW islanded plant costs ~$5B, but inference API revenue can yield ~$100B/GW/yr at 90%+ GM — so paying 2x or accepting 30% lower efficiency for speed is a no-brainer; Anthropic can pay back a power plant in ~20 days of inference revenue. The grid can't keep up (5+ yr to build generation; years to interconnect).

**Winners** are NOT just the incumbents [[GEV|GE Vernova]] and Siemens Energy: the biggest beneficiaries are the **~22 makers of reciprocating engines** (0.5-20 MW/unit — INNIO/Jenbacher, Caterpillar G3520, Cummins, Wartsila; up from 12 a year ago) and **solid-oxide fuel cells** ([[BE|Bloom]] carries 3.8 GW of the tracker's fuel-cell orders vs 0.03 GW for FuelCell Energy). Bloom is going vertical (install + O&M). Caterpillar is lifting large-recip output to ~3x 2024 levels; INNIO ~3.5→10 GW/yr by 2030. Named deployments: Microsoft 5 GW BTM YTD (2.7 GW Joulent/Chevron); Google 930 MW off-grid turbines + 900 MW Bloom fuel cells in Wyoming (SA called this out Feb-2026) + >1 GW Mitsubishi J-class; Anthropic/Meta 300-500 MW Enchanted Rock; OpenAI's 1.4 GW Shackelford TX site on 500+ Jenbacher J624s (>$150B contracted with Oracle).

**Why it's hard** — six gates: contract & bankability (the chicken-and-egg financing wall → rise of Energy-as-a-Service "BTM utilities" like VoltaGrid, Williams, Solaris), permitting (air-permit PTE thresholds, NSR/PSD; developers pick states like Texas, or straddle borders as xAI did for Colossus 2), fuel supply, equipment procurement, workforce (labor shortage), and the physics of running without a grid (step-load, supercaps for fuel cells). Cracks: permitting delays forced Oracle Project Jupiter and Nebius NJ to pivot to Bloom fuel cells; a secondary turbine market is emerging from failed/speculative orders — most exposed are turbine incumbents GEV and Siemens.
