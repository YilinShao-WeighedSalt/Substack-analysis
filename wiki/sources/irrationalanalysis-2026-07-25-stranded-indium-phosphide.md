# Stranded Indium-Phosphide

Follow-up to the author's earlier SOA piece. Core argument: the **indium-phosphide (InP)
shortage is structurally worse than the DRAM shortage**. An analogy from an industry contact:
"~25% of DRAM goes to AI — you can always take more from consumer. But ~80% of InP already
goes to AI, and InP has *zero* consumer use-case." You can metaphorically shoot the smartphone
industry and annex its DRAM; you cannot do that with InP.

The post then cost-models two ways to build an ELSFP module for NPO/CPO (single-wavelength
1310nm, 22 dBm/fiber, 8 fibers): the "normal" 8× discrete DFB approach vs. the "LiDAR shitco"
**SOA (Semiconductor Optical Amplifier)** approach. An SOA is a laser without gratings; only a
small number of ex-LiDAR firms know how to make high-power SOA because the incumbents
(Lumentum, Coherent, Broadcom, Sumitomo, Furukawa, AAOI) were "too busy making money in real
markets." Result: the normal way gets **51% gross margin** vs the SOA way at **17%** — but since
LiDAR is dead, those firms would take big low-margin revenue happily. "The math works."

On who can actually do it: only two firms have viable high-power SOA for NPO/CPO — one a private
IDM (unnamed), the other manufacturing via **Seivers as the InP fab partner**, with public claims
the author independently verified and used to build the COGS model. Public info strongly indicates
**Aeva uses Seivers**. The author is skeptical of Seivers' DWDM monolithic-array product (horrific
yield; Lumentum cut everyone except Nvidia off; Ayar removed from Lumentum's list, not vice-versa)
but is *mildly bullish Seivers* purely because of the Aeva SOA relationship — Seivers will make
more money off Aeva than it ever did off Ayar Labs. **"Aeva is a more interesting buy than
Seivers."**

Disclosure: author owns **Lumentum (LITE), AXTI, and AAOI** only; not buying Aeva ("not bothering
with this crap, but dogshit is fun to write about").
