# Semi Doped Daily Update — July 27th, 2026

**CXMT STAR-market debut +470%.** China's DRAM champion IPO'd (Shanghai's biggest ever, $9.8B),
closing ~49 yuan vs 8.66 offer, briefly China's largest A-share (¥3.6T / ~$533B), displacing ICBC.
Nomura projects a further +1,239% and DRAM revenue to $2T by 2030. CXMT is pricing 64GB server
DDR5 RDIMMs *above* Samsung — shifting from volume supplier to margin-seeking price-power player.
*Vik:* anyone with DRAM supply now commands pricing power (Apple absorbs the cost); CXMT will play
the margin game, not stay low-cost. *Austin:* CXMT has no EUV → DUV multipatterning → more steps,
overlay error, higher cost/bit; in a normal down-cycle the high-cost producer suffers first — but
CXMT is state-backed and can run unprofitably.

**Korea $950B AI summit.** Samsung + SK Group signed ~$950B of partnerships with US tech in SF.
**Broadcom awarded Samsung a $200B MoU** (foundry + HBM + packaging through 2030), reportedly
because "TSMC was too costly, too slow." **Nvidia–SK Group partnership >$500B** (AI factories +
long-term HBM), plus a **$1B Nvidia investment in Naver** (+10%). *Vik:* future HBM will use
increasingly custom base dies — making HBM a custom component, not a commodity, and a lever for
Nvidia to lock up Hynix supply.

**HBM "is not the answer."** SK Hynix's Hoshik Kim conceded at the Raise Summit (echoing
Gelsinger calling HBM "a lousy memory" / "ugly baby") that HBM will not solve the memory-wall
problem — an inherent AI problem (thermal, IO, cost); Hynix pursuing solutions beyond HBM.

**Quick hits:** Nvidia in talks for a ~$250B financing guarantee for OpenAI's Ohio DC; Microchip
to acquire edge-inference startup Hailo; **Infineon raised FY guidance** on AI-datacenter power
semis and opened the world's largest power fab in Dresden; **SK Hynix now Kioxia's de facto #2
holder** after Bain's record $17B stake sale; LG Innotek Q2 op profit +2,057% YoY on AI substrates;
AMD + Cerebras unveiled a disaggregated inference platform; Google Cloud +82% YoY, Alphabet capex
to $195–205B; BlackRock's $12.3B bond sale for Meta's Texas DC met weak demand (hyperscaler-debt
anxiety).
