SemiAnalysis's Energy Model team spent 6 months reverse-engineering PJM's "Reserve Requirement Study" (RRS) — the black-box model PJM uses to decide how much and what type of generation to buy in its annual capacity auction across America's largest electricity market (66M residents).

**Core claim:** PJM's methodology **undercounts ~4 GW of existing generation** by failing to account for higher power-plant efficiency in winter and improved resilience since Winter Storm Elliott (federally mandated cold-weather upgrades add ~3.8 GW of reliable capacity). This overstated the supply/demand shortfall, wasting **~$12B of ratepayer money 2025–2027** (~20% bill increases). Better modeling would have saved $6.7B while procuring only 14 MW less power (2025/26), then $4.9B / 0.8 GW (2026/27) — the disproportion arises because PJM operates at the razor's edge of its supply/demand curves.

**Structural critique:** PJM runs the world's only capacity market that doesn't distinguish new vs existing plants, so any growth premium is paid to *all* generators as windfall. One-year contracts starting too soon after signing + slow interconnection queues constrain new supply; an "emergency auction" for new generation is being stood up but signs contracts without committed counterparties, converting modeling errors into long-standing liabilities.
