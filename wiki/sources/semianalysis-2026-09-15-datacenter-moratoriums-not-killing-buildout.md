A parcel-by-parcel audit rebutting the "moratoriums are killing the US datacenter buildout" narrative. Thesis-level bullish AI infra; no fresh priced ticker.

**Findings.** Four states acted in <2 months (NY halted DC environmental permits, TX paused ERCOT interconnection, PA pulled fast-track permitting, OR froze state-land deals) and 300+ localities voted to halt DCs. But mapping 400+ instruments across 17 states to a 6,000-facility project pipeline (property records, permits, power data, FOIA, satellite, plus "an army of research agents"): only **~2.3GW is genuinely delayed** — ~1,525MW at local level (3 projects: an AWS Ohio campus, a PA powered-land developer, a Colorado site) + ~0.8GW from NY's order. SemiAnalysis's Datacenter Industry Model still forecasts **+38GW US IT capacity in 2027 (>2x 2026)**, 22GW under vertical construction.

**Why counting moratoriums ≠ counting megawatts.** For a moratorium to bind, all must hold at once: it reaches the parcel (county rules often stop at city lines), it freezes new applications rather than revoking approvals (so entitled projects proceed), it doesn't expire before the project needs the approval, and nothing else already gates the schedule. Brownsville "de-annexed" 444 acres days before approving Elon's DC. **The buildout is moving behind-the-meter (BtM)** — TX's grid delay is offset by BtM acceleration; SA counts 75GW of firm BtM orders (last week's Part 1), a net positive for on-site generation. With Nov-2026 elections, moratoriums are low-cost political signaling (13 statewide bills died in 2026).
