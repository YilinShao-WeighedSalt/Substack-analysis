Deep dive on **modular datacenter construction** (full body served unauthenticated, ~10.6k words). Thesis: how DCs are built now bears little resemblance to before — concrete panels, wired power rooms, and whole data halls arrive on trucks and get bolted together "like LEGO." The forcing function is a **trade-labor shortage** (electricians = 30-40% of DC man-hours; a shortage emerges 2027, worst in TX/OH) that capex alone can't solve; modular pulls repeatable work into factories in parallel with sitework, and **speed = revenue**.

Builds a taxonomy (prefabrication vs modular; the 5-rung ladder component -> skid -> module -> container -> prefab block), maps a **80+ vendor universe**, and rebuilds vendor speed/cost claims bottom-up against a 50MW liquid-cooled US baseline: **~36% faster (7-9 months), ~8% cheaper/MW** — but the real value is stranded-GPU-depreciation opportunity cost (~$0.5M/MW/mo, up to ~$4M/MW pulled forward). Three integration models: operator-led (AWS Houdini w/ Cupertino Electric=[[PWR]]; Aligned), EPC/integrator-led ([[FIX]] Comfort Systems, [[STRL]] Sterling), OEM-led ([[VRT]] Vertiv OneCore ~$3.5M->~$7M/MW; [[SU.PA]] Schneider). Nvidia **DSX** standardizes the whole AI-factory as an Omniverse digital twin. Estimates modular = 30%+ of live capacity by end-2028. Caveats: reliability doubts, double-margin, transport/superload limits, and on-site commissioning (3-8 mo) as the biggest gap. New theme page [[modular-datacenter-construction]].
