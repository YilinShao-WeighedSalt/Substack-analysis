Engineering read of Lumentum's Q4/FY26 call, which the author calls "ultra-bullish" — with the alpha buried by management's own poor messaging and the sell-side missing it entirely.

Core argument: Lumentum's laser advantage is real and widening, and the market is mis-framing the LITE-vs-COHR comparison purely on CapEx/FCF/GM/growth optics without understanding the physics underneath.

- **Disaggregated (external) lasers win.** A ring-modulated single-λ PIC split 6× needs ~2 mW into each ring → 400 mW external (ELS/ELSFP) vs 250 mW if integrated into the optical engine. High-power 400 mW lasers are ~65% as area-efficient as 250 mW, so amid the InP crunch some "big fish" are integrating mid-power lasers to save InP area — but integration is a *terrible* idea: harder to keep drive current clean / temperature stable, so mode hops (link-flap) rise; and if any laser dies the whole optical engine (and whatever it's soldered to) dies vs just swapping an ELSFP module. People only integrate because of the crippling photon/InP shortage. "Hilariously bullish for Lumentum."
- **The hidden moat = wide mode-hop-free range across temperature.** Doesn't show on a datasheet — only in 1K-sample stress qual. Co-integrating mid-power lasers with NPO engines makes this reliability metric *much* more important, and Lumentum leads it. Management (Wupen/Michael) had the differentiation but "catastrophically failed to explain why" (the key phrase: customer yields are better because of the wider mode-hop-free range of Lumentum CW lasers).
- **Margins/pricing.** UHP (ultra-high-power) laser GM 80–90%; ELS module GM 50–60%. Broadcom also sold out → Lumentum hiking prices into no real competition.
- **New engineering datapoint:** they *reduced cavity length again* while meeting the same linewidth requirement — "this entire call is so damn bullish."
