Jargon decoder

Parametric yield — the share of chips that hit target clock/power after defective dies are already discarded. It's an economic choice (where on the volt/freq curve you ship), not a design one — and the core of Irrational's Cerebras thesis.
Yield harvesting / binning — selling the slower dies as cheaper SKUs instead of scrapping them. Cerebras can't do this: 84 reticles are fused into one wafer, so a weak corner can't be downgraded or thrown away.
NVHBM — Micron's custom HBM for Nvidia where the base die (the logic layer under the DRAM stack) is Nvidia-designed and TSMC-built; the debate is whether the profit from it accrues to Micron or to Nvidia.
Base die — the logic chip at the bottom of an HBM stack that marshals bandwidth; as stacks shrink (12-hi→8-hi→4-hi) value shifts from the DRAM to whoever designs this die.
Circular financing — a vendor lends its customer the money to buy the vendor's own product (here AVGO lending Anthropic up to $42B to lease AVGO/Google chips). Boosts bookings now; concentrates risk on one balance sheet.
Securitization (of chips) — bundling owned GPUs and selling the cash-flow claim to outside investors (Amazon, ~$8B) to move the hardware off the balance sheet — AI capex turned into a financing instrument.
LogicFolding — Huawei's trick (Kirin 9050 Pro) of stacking transistors vertically instead of shrinking them, routing around the process-node ceiling that export controls were meant to enforce.
Snapdragon X2 — Qualcomm's 2nd-gen Arm PC chip; benchmarks beat Intel Core Ultra at equal/lower price, pressuring Intel's grip on premium Windows laptops into the 2027 refresh.
Czochralski (CZ) method — the 1916 crystal-pulling technique (~90% of all semiconductor wafers) behind TIL's history piece; a seed crystal drawn from a melt grows a single-crystal ingot.
