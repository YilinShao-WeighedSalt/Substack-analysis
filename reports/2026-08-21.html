Jargon decoder

CS-4 / WSE-3 — Cerebras' 4th-gen rack around the same dinner-plate-sized wafer chip; 3 wafers/rack (was 2) at 2× clock → ~2× tokens/sec/user at similar cost.
Parametric yield — the share of chips that hit target clock & power, not just "works." IA's short: Cerebras solved works-or-not (100%) but not fast-enough (~20%), capping gross margin.
Interactivity (tok/s/user) — how fast one user's answer streams. Cerebras' "ultrafast" tier claims ~4,000 vs a GPU's realistic ~100.
Disaggregated inference — splitting LLM prefill and decode across different hardware; CS-4's field-upgradeable I/O lets a wafer pair with HBM boxes to cover its tiny 44 GB SRAM.
CPO (co-packaged optics) — moving the optical engine into the chip package to beat the copper-interconnect bandwidth wall. SK Hynix just mapped a memory→optics roadmap.
SOA — semiconductor optical amplifier — boosts laser light on-die. Fine for one color; with many colors it spawns four-wave-mixing noise (Intel's CPO problem).
RIN — relative intensity noise — a laser's "flicker." Too much and the optical link fails; flagged on Intel's CPO and Nvidia/Lumentum's monolithic array.
ELSFP — external-laser pluggable module — laser kept outside the switch; IA argues this disaggregated design (Lumentum's) beats integration on reliability.
EMIB-T — Intel's packaging bridge, the "most credible CoWoS alternative"; picking up tape-outs as TSMC CoWoS fills.
CoWoS — TSMC's logic-plus-HBM advanced packaging; ~99% yield but capacity-full, so overflow reroutes to Samsung (+15% price) and Intel.
Custom HBM base die — HBM4 lets the memory stack's bottom logic layer be tailored per customer; the scarce input is design teams & tooling, not supply.
Circular financing — Nvidia guarantees the debt that funds the datacenter that buys Nvidia chips (the $105B Ohio backstop) — bullish for sales, a demand-risk tell.
